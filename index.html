My Role

I designed My eXp and MIRA end to end, from the problem and the information architecture to the UI, the component patterns, and MIRA’s conversational experience.

This is UX design work I did as a Senior UX / Product Designer at eXp Realty on the My eXp agent portal. MIRA and some other areas are shown as earlier prototype designs. I used Figma for wireframes, UI, and components; ChatGPT and Claude to synthesize research inputs, explore flows, and draft UX copy; and Lovable for the AI-assisted working prototype.

03

Problem

Agents were spending the day finding information instead of acting on it.

How might we give an agent one personal, consistent platform that answers “where do I stand, and what should I do next?”, and let them get there by simply asking?

An agent’s working day spans many questions: how close they are to their goals and their cap, what they have earned in revenue share and when they will be paid, which deal needs attention, who their mentor is, and where to find a document or get help. When those answers live in separate tools and dense tables, the work is hunting, not deciding.

The product vision: a unified real estate platform that combines the My eXp agent experience with MIRA, an intelligent AI assistant that helps agents access information, complete tasks, navigate workflows, and make better business decisions through natural conversations.

Scattered answers

Goals, cap, revenue share, deals, mentorship, documents, and help sit in different places.

Dense, hard-to-read data

Earnings and the revenue share organization are hierarchical and table-heavy.

No clear next step

It isn’t obvious which deal or task needs attention right now.

Inconsistent patterns

When every area behaves differently, the interface has to be relearned page by page.

Product goals

  • Personal: a Home that reflects each agent’s own goals and progress
  • Legible: make complex earnings and organization data easy to read
  • Actionable: surface what needs attention next
  • Consistent: one set of patterns across every area
  • Conversational: let agents ask MIRA instead of hunting through menus
  • Demo-ready: show many user states without separate accounts

Research Methods

The framework centers on the agent’s daily jobs and the questions they need answered. Participant counts and verbatim quotes are not part of this write-up.

Daily jobs

Questions before features

Mapped the working day to the questions agents actually ask: cap progress, what they earned and when they are paid, which deal needs attention, who their mentor is, and where a document or a person is. Key insight: answers were scattered, so agents spent effort finding information instead of acting on it.

ChatGPT and Claude supported synthesis, clustering inputs into five themes: scattered information, hard-to-read earnings, unclear next action, getting help, and growth and mentorship. Design decisions stayed with me.

09

Design Decisions

Each choice keeps the agent in context: a personal Home, legible earnings, one workspace per deal, and the same patterns everywhere.

A personalized Home that answers “where do I stand?”

Home is a modular set of tiles: a Getting Started checklist for new agents, Capping Year progress with GCI, Volume, Units, and Revenue Share, Scheduled Training, Achievements, What’s New, and Upline Partners. Home changes with the agent’s stage. The Getting Started checklist leads for new joiners and drops away after the first 90 days.

Customize Home Screen lets each agent drag tiles into the order they want, with Reset to Default, Cancel, and Save Changes, and a short first-run overlay that explains how. Trade-off: customize mode is another state to learn, so the overlay and Reset to Default are the way out.

Before: a light sidebar and a mission-control layout of goal KPIs (Homes Closed, Commission, Sales Volume), Weekly Quests, and a capping ring. After: dark navy navigation, a full-width announcement banner, and the modular tiles above. Source for the current Home: Home-Screens Figma file.

Capping progress as a moment, not a number

Capping is one of the most meaningful milestones for an agent, so it is a recurring visual thread: the Capping Year progress bar on Home, a year-by-year capping history timeline on the Agent dashboard, and a CAP REACHED celebration state that turns goals green and adds goal-reached badges. Trade-off: the celebration is a scenario state, not the everyday layout, so the default Home stays calm until the milestone is real.

RevShare, split into jobs an agent can finish

RevShare data is dense and hierarchical. I split it into clear jobs. My Earnings opens on Revenue Share with My FLA, My FLQA, My New Agents, and My Prospective Agents cards, a Revenue Share Group donut with agent counts per level, and Unpaid, Expected Next, and Last Paid amounts that each open an agent-level table. The Revenue Share Group report lists every agent with level, cap percentage, and amount, and a side panel for details.

Organization Reporting separates an Overview Dashboard (KPI tiles, year-to-date revenue share and volume, agent mix, and per-level charts), an Off-boarding Overview, and a Detail Report. Large reports download in the background and land in Notifications, with a clear state when a report is too large to display. Trade-off: more screens than one earnings page, and each screen answers one question instead of stacking every chart.

Current earnings, payout, group, and organization screens are from the Cosmo Transition and Organization Figma files.

Agent Desk: one workspace per transaction

Agent Desk starts with the active pipeline and a “Needs your attention” list so the next action is obvious. Each transaction opens into a workspace with persistent key facts (agent, buyer, expected close, days on market, price) and tabs for Overview, Contacts, Commission, Checklist, Documents, Activity, and Comments. Trade-off: seven tabs are more structure than one long page, and they keep commission, documents, and comments from collapsing into a single dense view.

One table pattern

Much of the portal is tabular, so one data-table pattern covers it: search and filter chips, a column manager, Save View, Download, per-column search, pagination, and row click to open a detail drawer. Once an agent learns it, it works the same on State Mentors, Production Details, Team Reconciliation, Revenue Share Group, and My Mentees. Trade-off: the column manager and Save View are more controls than a plain table, and they remove the need to relearn each report.

Team dashboard, simplified to the numbers that move

Before: team production, onboarding agents, Top Agents tabs, and Mega Team re-qualification progress. After: Units, Volume, and Team Lead Split tiles with pending values, a currency selector and Filter, and Top Agents tabs for Units Sold and Highest Volume. Source: Cosmo Transition Figma file. Trade-off: some of the earlier re-qualification detail is not on this dashboard; the first screen stays readable.

Notifications with context

Notifications became a full page with an Activity Feed and a Recognition tab, quick date and type filters, and a detail drawer that explains the event in context (capping details, ICON status, top transaction). Recognition items include a send-a-gift action so agents can celebrate peers. The current design simplifies the list: one Filter button, category tags, and inline actions such as “Say Congrats!” and “Invite to a Training.” Source: Cosmo Transition Figma file. Trade-off: the current list drops some of the earlier filter chrome in favor of actions on the row.

Mentor Program flows for every role and state

Mentorship has many states, so it is a set of journeys. A new agent sees a Find Your Mentor card with a countdown, compares mentor cards and profiles, or lets the system choose. Agents apply to become mentors through a prefilled multi-step application. Certified mentors get a dashboard for mentees, pending requests (Accept / Decline), and their own public profile. Mentees see their mentor, training materials, and progress. Key flows stay in context: the application is Start, prefilled from profile, multi-step form, Submit. Trade-off: more screens than a single Mentor page, and each role lands in the right state instead of a generic form.

Demo Config, so one prototype tells several stories

To demo realistic journeys without fake accounts, Demo Configuration sits in the account menu: mentor modes, capping scenarios (including CAP REACHED), ICON toggles, country, and FLQA states. Switching a scenario updates the live UI. A sidebar Demo Toggles menu covers the common switches during a walkthrough. The same prototype can show a new agent looking for a mentor, an agent who just capped, and a certified mentor in one session. Trade-off: demo controls are a designer and stakeholder tool, not an agent-facing feature, so they live in the account menu rather than in the everyday nav.

MIRA, beyond a chatbot

MIRA is designed to go beyond a traditional chatbot. Rather than a help widget that answers FAQs, it is an assistant that understands what the agent is trying to do, uses the context of their business, and helps them act, with the agent always in control.

In the prototype

  • Floats on every screen, one click away from Home and every other area
  • Opens with a greeting, suggested prompts (GCI trends, listing velocity, active pipeline), and chat history
  • Answers with chart insight cards that can be pinned to the dashboard
  • The same MIRA panel powers AI chat in Contact eXpert Care, so help and insights share one entry point

Conversation principles

  • Start with prompts, not a blank box
  • Show, don’t tell: a short summary and a chart insight card
  • Pin an insight card so the answer is useful later
  • Chat history keeps earlier questions close
  • Plain language: UX copy drafted with ChatGPT and Claude, then edited for tone
  • Always a way out: call or email in Contact eXpert Care

When a request is unclear or fails, MIRA asks a clarifying question, offers options, or routes to a human in Contact eXpert Care.

Outcome

What the work produced, what it taught, and what is still ahead. No business-impact numbers were recorded in the source case study.

What shipped in the prototype

  • A stateful Lovable prototype, first built as 71 screens and states, now a walkthrough of 77 with 32 brought up to the current design
  • One MIRA assistant across the platform: the same panel serves insights and Contact eXpert Care
  • One table pattern reused on five data-heavy pages
  • Demo Config, so one prototype can tell the story of a new agent, an agent who just capped, and a certified mentor in the same session

Handoff package

  • Figma screens, components, and their states
  • The Lovable prototype as a reference for real interaction behavior
  • Interaction notes for drawers, tabs, tables, and multi-step flows
  • MIRA conversation specs: intents, response formats, confirmation steps, and error and recovery states
  • Demo Config scenarios for QA and stakeholder review
  • Accessibility notes for color, contrast, and keyboard flows

Learnings

Working across Figma and Lovable changed how I design. Figma gave a clear structure and a shared set of components. Building those designs in Lovable with AI assistance made it possible to move from idea to a working, stateful experience quickly, and to test drawers, drill-downs, and multi-step flows as real behavior instead of static frames.

Designing MIRA taught me that an AI assistant is a product surface of its own. It needs intents, response formats, confirmation, and recovery designed as carefully as any screen. ChatGPT and Claude were useful thinking partners for exploring flows and tightening UX copy, while the design decisions stayed mine.

Demo Config turned out to be one of the most useful design tools in the project. One prototype can tell the story of a new agent looking for a mentor, an agent who just capped, and a certified mentor, live in the same session.

Future roadmap

  • Usability testing with agents on Home, RevShare, Agent Desk, and MIRA
  • Complete the remaining routes: Events Calendar, Tools, Knowledge Base, Downloads
  • Mobile layouts for the most frequent on-the-go tasks
  • Accessibility review of color, contrast, and keyboard flows in charts and tables
  • Deeper MIRA capabilities grounded in the agent’s own data
  • MIRA task execution through AI agents, with human confirmation for important actions
